Family Caregiving Checklist: How Siblings Can Share Care Without Dropping the Ball

A short, practical checklist for a family care plan — for siblings and relatives who are splitting the care of a parent or relative. Agree on roles, list the recurring work, give every task one named owner, hand tasks off properly, and keep one shared timeline everyone can see. Print it, fill it in together, and pin it somewhere the whole family will actually look.

1Agree on roles before you start

Most caregiver conflict between siblings comes from unclear authority, not unclear love. Decide this as a family, in writing, before a crisis forces the decision for you.

  • Pick one person as the day-to-day coordinator for scheduling and decisions.
  • Decide who has medical/legal power of attorney, if anyone does, and keep that separate from day-to-day coordination.
  • Agree how the family will make a shared decision when siblings disagree (majority, coordinator's call, or a family meeting).
  • Write down who is not available for regular tasks (distance, work, health) so expectations are realistic.
  • Set a recurring check-in (weekly or biweekly call, or a shared summary) so nobody has to chase updates.

2List every recurring task

Write down the actual recurring work, not just "help mom." A task that isn't written down has no owner, and a task with no owner becomes one person's job by default.

  • Medical and dental appointments — booking, driving, and attending.
  • Medication pickups and refill reminders.
  • Bills and paperwork — rent/mortgage, utilities, insurance, benefits forms.
  • Grocery runs and meal prep.
  • Regular visits or phone/video check-ins.
  • Home maintenance and safety checks.
  • Transport to social activities, worship, or appointments.

3Give every task one named owner

"We'll figure it out" is how appointments get missed. Every recurring task on your list from step 2 should have exactly one person's name next to it — not "whoever is free."

  • Go through the list from step 2 and write one name next to each task.
  • If a task has no obvious owner, decide together instead of letting it default to whoever asked first.
  • Rebalance the list if one person ends up with most of the recurring tasks.
  • Revisit ownership every few months as work, health, or availability changes.

4Hand a task over properly

Plans change — someone gets sick, travels, or has a work deadline. The failure mode isn't handing a task off; it's handing it off by text and assuming the other person saw it. A real handover needs an explicit yes.

  • Tell the new owner directly what the task is and when it's due — don't just drop it in a group chat.
  • Get an explicit "yes, I've got it" before you stop tracking the task yourself.
  • If the other person can't take it, agree on a backup instead of leaving it unowned.
  • Confirm once the handed-off task is actually done.

5Keep one shared timeline

Appointments, visits, and deadlines that live in one sibling's personal calendar are invisible to everyone else. Put them somewhere every caregiver can see.

  • Put every appointment, visit, and deadline in one place the whole family can see — not five separate calendars.
  • Note who is attending each appointment, not just when it is.
  • Keep a simple running history of what's been done, so a sibling who was away can catch up in minutes.
  • Share a short weekly or monthly recap with anyone who isn't checking daily.
This checklist is a coordination tool for organizing family caregiving logistics. It is not medical advice — for any health or treatment decision, talk to the person's doctor or care team.

How TaskKin Care supports this checklist

This checklist works with pen and paper. TaskKin Care is an iPhone and iPad app that keeps the same five habits going day to day, so ownership and handoffs stay clear without another group chat.

01

One named owner per task

Every task has a single owner, matching step 3 — no task quietly becomes "whoever is free."

02

Handoffs the other person must accept

Handing off a task creates a request to the new owner, who has to accept it before ownership moves — matching step 4's "get an explicit yes."

03

Three roles for step 1

Coordinator, Caregiver, and Viewer roles put the authority decision from step 1 directly into the app.

04

Relatives join with a code, no account needed

A relative can join a family's care circle with a 6-digit code or a QR code and a display name — no account or password required, so getting everyone in from step 1 takes a minute.

05

One shared timeline

Appointments, visits, and events sit in a single family timeline, matching step 5.

06

An append-only audit trail

Key coordination changes — tasks, handoffs, role changes — are written to an append-only log. The app only lets members add entries; it gives them no way to edit or delete one, so the running history from step 5 stays trustworthy.

A document you upload — like an appointment letter or a bill — can also be scanned on the device itself: text recognition runs locally (Apple Vision on iOS, Google ML Kit on Android), and the file's contents aren't sent to an external OCR service.
